For pharmacies & healthcare providers

Counselling support, built from the Needs Analysis

01

Check before crushing or splitting

Confirm whether a score line is for dosing or just easier swallowing, and whether a modified-release or coated tablet is safe to crush at all — the single most-prioritised topic in both countries' surveys.

02

Time disposal guidance to the moment

Offer take-back guidance at therapy change, at treatment completion, and when a patient is clearing a deceased relative's medicines — the points the Needs Analysis found disposal advice is most needed and least often given.

03

Flag leftover antibiotics specifically

Part-used courses and children's antibiotic syrups are commonly kept "for next time." Remind patients to return them — antibiotic syrups also carry a short shelf life once opened.

For patients & caregivers

Five rules for medicines at home

Your pharmacist gave you this code because these are exactly the questions ECO CARE was built to answer.

01

Ask before crushing

Not every tablet can be split or crushed safely — check with your pharmacist first.

02

Measure liquid doses properly

Use the syringe or cup that came with the medicine, never a kitchen spoon.

03

Store it out of reach

Cool, dry, out of light, and out of reach of children — not the bathroom cabinet.

04

Finish or return antibiotics

Never save leftovers "just in case," and never share them with someone else.

05

Return, don't bin or flush

Unused or expired medicines go back to a pharmacy — never the bin, sink, or toilet.

Frequently asked

Common questions

Can I crush my tablet to make it easier to swallow?

Sometimes — but not always. Ask your pharmacist before you do.

What do I do with leftover antibiotics?

Don't keep them for later and don't share them.

Why can't I just bin old medicines or flush them?

Medicines flushed or binned can end up in soil and waterways.

Where do I return unused or expired medicines?

In Slovenia, community pharmacies and collection centres accept waste medicines. In Croatia, pharmacies collect them under the national take-back system.

Is this the same as my doctor's or pharmacist's instructions?

It supports their advice, not replaces it.
